--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

New York City is one of the most visited cities in the world, attracting over
65 million tourists annually. Airbnb has become a significant part of the city's
accommodation landscape, with nearly 49,000 active listings as of 2019. This
report analyses the NYC Airbnb Open Data to uncover patterns in pricing,
geographic distribution, host behaviour, and guest preferences.

The goal is to provide actionable insights for three key audiences:
  - Prospective and existing Airbnb hosts seeking to optimise their listings
  - Policy makers evaluating the platform's impact on housing and tourism
  - Airbnb as a platform looking to grow and regulate its NYC market

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA OVERVIEW AND METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source Data:
  - 48,895 Airbnb listings across all five NYC boroughs
  - 16 attributes per listing: location, price, room type, host details,
    reviews, availability, and minimum night requirements

Cleaning Steps Applied:
  - Filled missing listing names (16 records) and host names (21 records)
    with placeholder values
  - Imputed reviews_per_month with 0 for listings with no reviews (10,052)
  - Removed 11 listings with price = $0 (likely data entry errors)
  - Excluded 53 listings with minimum_nights > 365 (long-term leases)
  - Converted date fields to proper datetime format

Feature Engineering:
  - Price categories: Budget, Economy, Mid-Range, Premium, Luxury
  - Host types: Individual (1 listing), Small Portfolio (2-5), Professional (6+)
  - Estimated annual revenue using the review-to-booking conversion method

Final working dataset: 48,831 listings

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. KEY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Geographic Distribution
............................

Manhattan and Brooklyn together account for approximately 85% of all listings.
The five most listing-dense neighbourhoods are:

    Rank  Neighbourhood           Borough      Listings
    1     Williamsburg            Brooklyn      3,920
    2     Bedford-Stuyvesant      Brooklyn      3,714
    3     Harlem                  Manhattan     2,658
    4     Bushwick                Brooklyn      2,465
    5     Upper West Side         Manhattan     1,971

The outer boroughs -- Bronx (1,091 listings, ~2.2%) and Staten Island
(373 listings, ~0.8%) -- are dramatically underrepresented. This reflects
both genuine tourist demand patterns and potential gaps in market coverage.

3.2 Pricing Dynamics
.....................

The median nightly price across NYC is $106, while the mean is $152,
indicating a strongly right-skewed distribution driven by high-end listings.

Price by Borough (Median):
    Manhattan:      $150
    Brooklyn:       $90
    Queens:         $75
    Staten Island:  $75
    Bronx:          $65

Price by Room Type (Median):
    Entire home/apt:  $160
    Private room:     $70
    Shared room:      $45

Approximately 50% of all listings fall in the Budget ($0-50) and Economy
($51-100) categories, signalling intense competition in the affordable
segment. Only about 5% of listings exceed $500/night.

3.3 Room Type Composition
..........................

    Room Type          Percentage
    Entire home/apt    52.0%
    Private room       45.7%
    Shared room         2.3%

Manhattan has a notably higher proportion of entire-home listings compared
to other boroughs. Brooklyn leans more toward private rooms, reflecting its
residential character and the prevalence of brownstone conversions.

3.4 Host Landscape and Market Concentration
...........................................

Individual hosts (one listing each) make up the majority of the host base.
However, professional hosts with 6 or more listings control a disproportionate
share of total listings.

While exact percentages depend on the filter criteria, the general pattern is
consistent: a small group of commercial operators holds significant market
power. The top 15 hosts alone manage hundreds of listings each, often spanning
multiple boroughs. This concentration raises questions about:
  - Impact on local housing supply and affordability
  - Compliance with short-term rental regulations
  - Fair competition for individual hosts

3.5 Review Patterns and Guest Engagement
.........................................

Review activity shows strong growth through 2019, with June and July being
peak months -- aligning with NYC's summer tourism season. Key observations:

  - Higher-priced listings tend to have fewer reviews, suggesting either
    lower booking frequency or a different guest profile
  - Listings with more reviews maintain higher visibility and booking rates
  - The average listing has approximately 23 reviews

3.6 Minimum Night Requirements
..............................

The vast majority of listings have a minimum stay of 1-3 nights. However,
roughly 4-5% of listings require 30 or more nights, effectively operating
as medium-to-long-term rentals. These listings may be:
  - Hosts circumventing NYC's short-term rental restrictions
  - Legitimate long-term rental offerings
  - Properties in transition between rental strategies

3.7 Revenue Estimates
.....................

Using a conservative estimation model (based on review-to-booking ratios),
Manhattan listings generate the highest median estimated annual revenue,
followed by Brooklyn. The top revenue-generating neighbourhoods closely
mirror the areas with the highest listing density and tourist traffic.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FOR NEW HOSTS:
  1. Target the budget-to-mid-range price segment ($50-200/night), where
     demand is strongest and competition, while fierce, offers volume.
  2. Brooklyn neighbourhoods such as Williamsburg and Bedford-Stuyvesant
     provide good demand with somewhat less saturation than core Manhattan.
  3. Use descriptive, appealing listing names. Words like "cozy", "private",
     "spacious", "modern", and "bright" appear frequently in successful
     listings.
  4. Set minimum nights to 1-2 to maximise booking potential.

FOR EXISTING HOSTS:
  1. Prioritise generating reviews -- they directly impact search visibility
     and guest trust. Consider follow-up messages encouraging guests to
     leave feedback.
  2. Increase year-round availability. Listings available for more days
     tend to perform better in search rankings.
  3. Regularly benchmark pricing against comparable listings in your
     neighbourhood and room-type segment.
  4. Consider offering discounts for longer stays to improve occupancy
     during off-peak months.

FOR POLICY MAKERS:
  1. Monitor the concentration of listings among professional hosts,
     especially entire-home listings in residential areas.
  2. Investigate listings with 30+ night minimums that may be skirting
     short-term rental regulations.
  3. The high density of listings in certain neighbourhoods (Williamsburg,
     Harlem, Bushwick) warrants assessment of the impact on local housing
     markets and community character.
  4. Consider requiring hosts with multiple listings to register as
     commercial operators.

FOR THE AIRBNB PLATFORM:
  1. The outer boroughs (Bronx, Staten Island) represent underserved
     markets. Promotional campaigns highlighting attractions in these
     areas could drive diversification.
  2. Develop tools to help individual hosts compete with professional
     operators (pricing suggestions, marketing support, etc.).
  3. The strong review activity growth signals healthy platform engagement
     in NYC, but the high concentration of professional operators could
     undermine trust if not managed transparently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Limitations:
  - The dataset is a snapshot from 2019 and does not capture post-pandemic
    changes in the NYC short-term rental market.
  - Revenue estimates are approximations based on review frequency, not
    actual booking data.
  - The dataset lacks guest demographics, seasonality details, and
    cancellation rates.

Future Work:
  - Incorporate more recent data to assess pandemic recovery patterns.
  - Build predictive models for pricing optimisation.
  - Analyse the impact of NYC's Local Law 18 (2023) on listing counts
    and host behaviour.
  - Integrate external data (neighbourhood demographics, transit access,
    points of interest) for richer geographic analysis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The NYC Airbnb market is large, dynamic, and geographically concentrated.
Manhattan and Brooklyn dominate both supply and revenue, while the outer
boroughs remain largely untapped. The pricing landscape is competitive,
with most listings falling in the budget-to-mid-range segment. Perhaps most
notably, a small number of professional hosts control a significant portion
of the market, raising important questions about the platform's role in
urban housing and the sharing economy more broadly.

This analysis provides a data-grounded foundation for hosts, regulators,
and platform operators to make informed decisions about their strategies
in one of the world's most important short-term rental markets.
